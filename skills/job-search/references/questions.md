# Asking the questions

In an attended turn, a question that has choices goes through Claude Code's question tool (AskUserQuestion), never a list in prose; `J ask <kind>` prints the exact payload to pass as the tool's input. Never in an unattended turn: nobody can answer there. Hold the job, send the notification and ask when the person is back (`J ask` refuses in an unattended batch).

## The shape

- Each option has a short label and a one-line description. The default is the first option, labelled "(Recommended)", or "(from your resume)" for a resume guess. No question or option text names the tool's own free-text option; code refuses one that does.
- Group the questions of one moment in one call: 1 to 4 questions, 2 to 4 options each.
- The start: the resume as a plain question (the `next` text of `J start status`), then one call (`J ask start`) with the apply mode, the search frequency, the positions and where to apply, then the `/rc` reminder if it prints one (not a question). An auto mode asks `J ask auto` next.
- Many sheets at once, attended: `J ask approve`, then `J ask approve-pick` for the groups of 12.
- The same tool for: the role, place or remote when the resume leaves a choice between two clear tracks (`J ask resume`), the Tenjin ask (`J ask tenjin`), the optional-writing `skip` or `wait` (`J ask optional-writing`), the attended yes for a sheet when it fits (`J ask yes`), the contacts count (`J ask contacts`), the follow-up (`J ask follow_up`) and the email batch (`J ask email`, over 16 drafts "Pick in groups of 16" with `--group <n>`).

## Choices only when the options hold the answer

- A typed answer is a plain question with your guess in its text: a place the resume does not give or a role with no clear guess (`J ask place`, `J ask role`), and a free-text fact such as a phone, pay, an address or a name.
- Form facts come from one command, `J ask facts`, never from a list you write: code collects the missing facts of every job prepared in that moment and merges the same question (the same normalized label, or the same profile fact: current company, current title, school, start date, notice period, work authorization, and the like) across jobs, so one answer fills every job that asks it. A fact the resume, the profile or an earlier answer already gives is filled with no question. A question with fixed options is a choice with the form's own options, word for word (`calls`, at most 4 questions each, made one after another in the same attended turn): 2 or 3 options all, plus a skip; 4 options all; more than 4 the first three and the form's decline option when it has one (else the fourth), never an option named Other. An answer the person types instead is recorded the same way and matched to each form's full list; a form it does not match is asked on its own; forms with different option lists show the most common list, and a form that does not hold it is asked on its own, never guessed. Dates, names and free text come in one plain message (`plain`). Each question names its jobs (the first few and a count). Record an answer with `J facts answer <id> <the chosen option or their words>` (it fills every job the question names and saves the earlier answer), a skip with `J facts skip <id>`. Facts and approvals never share a call. In a turn the watcher woke nothing is asked: the jobs hold and one push says how many questions wait.
- A rule is a question-tool call too ("Skip clearance and citizenship-restricted jobs?"): never ask for exact words in prose. Record the chosen option with `J rules add --from-choice "<the option label>" --effect <e> --value <v>`: the label is the person's words, with the date. Text typed as the answer goes in `--words`.
- A form's own legal terms are their own question (`J ask acknowledge`), with the form's text and link.
- The voluntary demographic fields are never a choice question and never go through the question tool (`J ask eeo` is refused): one plain question at the first form that has them, built by `J eeo question --key <job>`, naming that form's fields and saying the person can answer each in their own words or say decline. The answer is saved as text (`J eeo set`). On later forms the saved text is matched to the form's own options, which is judgment in the skill, and recorded with `J eeo answer`; a field the text does not answer is asked again plainly, for that field only (`J eeo add`), never guessed. The question marks an optional field as optional and says it can be skipped (`J eeo skip <key> <field>`); a required field cannot be skipped.
- The follow-up is "Did you hear back?"; after "They replied" one optional plain question asks what they said (`J ask follow_up_detail`).

## Tenjin hints

Code computes a `hint` on a work item or report row at these moments, so the model never decides when to mention Tenjin: a search round while Tenjin is missing, outdated or unfunded; a job whose free steps found no employer posting; a job whose free ways found no contact; a LinkedIn link given as the resume. Say the hint's line once.

The ask comes at most once per session (`askTenjin` on the item) and is built by code (`tenjinOffer`): the current moment's hint line first, then what Tenjin adds with each capability's own price, then the pace line from the person's search frequency. Every figure comes from `prices` in `graph.json` (the recorded provider price, plus the 15% routing fee only for a routed query, never for a call by catalog tool id, rounded up to a tenth of a cent, with the date it was checked), so no price is quoted here. Say the ask and a hint exactly as `J` prints them; the options are "Set up Tenjin (Recommended)" and "Not now" (`J ask tenjin --json '{"line","status"}'`). On a yes, follow the "Install Tenjin from tenjin.sh" flow at tenjin.sh/skills.md, which includes the funding step; if Tenjin is there with no balance, run the CLI's own `tenjin fund`. Pick no amount, change no Tenjin setting, name no seller and no model. Record the answer with `J tenjin ask-done yes|no`; a no is kept with its date, and the ask waits for the next session. In an unattended run, only mention it in the report.

## Referrals
- A job at a company where the person might get a referral is not submitted unattended before they have had the chance. The skill never reads LinkedIn connections and never opens LinkedIn: the referral is a question, one per company, asked at the first job at each company (only in an auto mode, where it can matter), merged across the companies waiting at that moment: `J ask referral` builds it ("Someone can refer me (I'll wait for it)", "No one, apply as usual", "Skip this job"). Record `J referral answer <company> someone|none|skip`. After "Someone", one plain question asks the referrer's name, a fact the person gives (`J referral name <company> <name>`). "No one" is saved per company and never asked again. Key people the contacts flow found are not referrers unless the person names one.
- Unattended, a job at a company whose question is open holds as `referral_check`, and one whose referral is pending as `referral_pending`; `J may-submit` refuses both. Each is asked at the next attended turn, with one push under the notify rules. An attended approval shows the pending referral and offers "Wait for the referral" beside the usual options; a fact and an approval never share a call.
- After 7 days (a timing default; the person changes it in their own words, the `referral_delay_days` rule) `J ask referral` also builds "Is your referral at <company> in?": "Yes, apply now" (`J referral in <company>`), "Not yet" (`J referral notyet <company>`), "Apply without it" (`J referral without <company>`).
- A form's referral field ("Referred by", or "How did you hear" with an employee option) is filled with the referrer's name the person gave, as the person's fact, never guessed; it wins over the where-you-heard mapping. A company that uses a link its employee sends: the person applies through it and says so (`J submitted <key> --referral-link`).
- `J referral draft <company>` gives the one writer call for a short request to the referrer, saved with `J referral draft-save <company> --text <file>` and shown as a draft. The person sends it; the skill never does.
- "Also worked at" hints: `J contacts warm` takes each past employer of the person (their own titles and dates, from the resume or a LinkedIn read with titles). For a past employer whose employee range tops out at 50 or fewer, one people search finds people now at the target who also worked there. For a bigger one, each such candidate is read once, and a hint shows only when code finds overlapping dates at that employer and a team word in both titles after the generic job words are removed (no titles or dates: no hint). Each is a contact marked "also worked at <company>", never contacted by the skill and never a referral unless the person says so.
