# Apply

Only the employer's own application page (`exact` or `confirmed`), on a host off the deny list: gate 4 in SKILL.md names the list and the stops (an account or sign-in, a bot check). Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby only have code adapters (questions, board checks); they are not a list of where to apply. The first application on a new host shows once in `J report --all`. Applications run in parallel, one job per host at a time.

**Why "apply yourself".** The report gives one of four reasons: the host is on the deny list, the form needs an account, a bot check appeared, or no employer posting was found.

## Approvals, fill-only and opening pages
- **The apply modes** (`J start record --mode`): attended (default), `auto_no_new` and `auto_write` (the two auto modes, asked in the auto follow-up `J ask auto`), `fill_only` ("Fill only, I submit": every job is filled and read back, never submitted, and a prepared job is filled with no per-job yes; the tab waits as `fill_ready` until the person says they submitted it) and `open_only` ("Just open the pages").
- **Approvals come only from code.** `J ask approval --key <job>` (one job) or `J ask approve` (many) builds every approval question, so it always has the fill-only option: "Yes, apply (Recommended)", "Fill it, I submit" and "Not this one" (a job on LinkedIn and on an employer site gets the four-option variant). Never write an approval question yourself. A form's own attestation ("I confirm the information is accurate") is a line of the sheet that the approval covers. A form's legal terms are their own question, `J ask acknowledge --json '{"company","text","link"}'`.
- **Order in a call.** A job's fact questions and legal terms come first, in their own call; its approval comes after every one is answered, so the sheet it shows is complete. Approvals for several jobs may share one call when every sheet is complete; an approval never shares a call with a fact or a terms question. Save facts with `J approve <key> --facts <json>` (or `J fact set`), which does not approve.
- **Record the answer:** `J approve <key>` ("Yes, apply"), `J approve <key> --fill-only` ("Fill it, I submit": the yes for that sheet hash with fill-only on for that job only), or `--via employer|site`. Many: `J ask approve`, then `J approve --all` or `--all --fill-only`; "Pick in groups of 12" is `J ask approve-pick` (three tick questions and "Apply them (Recommended)" or "Fill them, I submit"). Say the one state line `J approve` prints, as given; never say you are "recording a yes" without it. With Claude in Chrome off, the same reply gives the steps to turn it on.
- **Withdraw.** When the person says stop, don't apply or just fill, the first step is `J approve <key|--all> --withdraw` or `J fillonly <key> on`, before anything else.
- **Just open the pages.** `J open <key...>` (attended turns only) runs the operating system's own open command with Google Chrome named as the app (`open -a "Google Chrome"` on macOS, the matching command on Linux and Windows; never the Claude in Chrome extension or its tools) with the job's exact https URL; Chrome opens it in its most recently used window and profile. If Chrome is missing or the command fails, the page opens in the default browser and the reply says so in one plain line; the link is in the reply either way. It refuses local and private addresses and allows a deny-listed host, because it only opens the page: nothing reads, fetches, fills or clicks it. Show each opened job's sheet as text so the person can copy the answers; the outcome is their word or "Did you apply?", recorded with `J submitted <key>`. It is also the fallback in every stronger mode for a job the skill must not or cannot fill (a deny-listed host, a sign-in, a bot check), in place of a link in the report. A turn the watcher wakes holds these as `open_ready`.
- **Claude in Chrome.** At every start record `J chrome set on` when the Claude in Chrome tools are in the session (listed or deferred) and `off` when they are not, again after a restart or a `/chrome` reconnect. With `off`, options that need Chrome carry a note. `on` clears the `chrome_disconnected` and `no_chrome_tools` holds.

## Open the batch
0. An auto mode hands out nothing until the referral list is answered (`referral_list_unanswered`), and a job at a listed company with a pending referral (`referral_pending`) is not handed out unattended (references/questions.md); an attended yes is not held.
1. `J batch open` (add `--unattended` for an unattended batch; `--upload` only names the resume the person gave). `apply next` hands out a job only in an open batch, once: a job already handed out, or past its last check, is not handed out again. Open the batch only after every prepare step of the round (`J sheet build`, the EEO match, answers and facts) is done: an unattended batch refuses the commands that record a yes, change a sheet or lift a stop.
2. Attended: every job the skill plans to apply to is already prepared, with no question before it (references/prepare.md). The first question the person gets about a job is its approval, with the prepared application shown: `J ask approval --key <job>` (the fields, any new answer in full, the warnings; a missing fact, such as US citizenship, in the same call with its fixed answers and "Skip this job"). Save the answers and record the yes with `J approve <key> --facts <json>`: the sheet is completed and the yes covers the completed sheet hash. Many sheets: `J ask approve`, then `J approve --all|--no-new|<keys>`. A changed sheet needs a new yes. In an auto mode nobody is asked: a missing fact holds the job as `needs_facts`.
3. `J apply next --all` (add `--unattended`) hands out every job that passes the checks, one per host (`handouts`); the rest wait in the `queue` as `host_busy` until their host is free. `J apply next` alone hands out one. Start the browser subagents for the whole set in one message as parallel Agent calls, each in its own tab. Each job keeps its own claim, its own `J may-submit` check and its own stops: a stop on one job (a bot check, an upload block, a sign-in) leaves the others going, except a Chrome disconnect and an unattended CAPTCHA, which close the round; when a round closes, `J may-submit` says no for every open job, so nothing more submits and the forms stay as they are. The round's one writer call (`J write-prompt --round`) stays a single call. Run `J apply next --all` again as hosts free up. The `tenjin mcp` needs of a search round and the board API reads still run in turn.

## Where the person applies (a job site)
The start asks where the person prefers to apply, stored as `apply.where`: the employer's site, a job site, or ask each job. One option covers every job site where a job can be applied to (LinkedIn, Indeed, YC job pages, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Wellfound and the like); there is no per-site question. The deny list still bars the browser automation, fetches for applying, fills and sign-ins on those sites, but opening a page for the person is allowed: for a job with a job-site link (`siteLinks`, one per site, in the order the job's links came, so the site it was found on first leads) under the job-site choice, `J site show <key>` runs `J open` on the first site's page in an attended turn, with no question, and prints the prepared answers (the sheet when an employer form was found, otherwise the profile facts and a short note you draft from the resume, facts only from the profile) and the job's other job-site links. Show them in the reply; the link is always in the reply. The skill does nothing on that site: no read, fetch, fill, click or sign-in. The site's own questions are the person's. In a turn the watcher woke it opens nothing and the job holds as `open_ready`; the next attended turn opens it. A job with no job-site link takes the employer's site under the apply mode. Under "Ask me each job", a job on a job site and on an employer site gets the choice in its approval call (`J ask approval --key <job>`): "Employer's site (I apply)" is `J approve <key> --via employer`, the job site's own name (for example "LinkedIn (you apply)") is `J approve <key> --via site`, "Skip this job" skips it; in an unattended turn it waits. The opened page and answers count toward `period_max`, the same as a sheet brought for a yes. At the next attended turn ask once, `J ask applied --key <job>` ("Did you apply to ... on <the site>?": "Yes", "Not yet", "Skip this job"), then `J site applied <key> yes|not_yet|skip`: applied is recorded, with `source: person`, only on the person's word, and "Not yet" is asked again only when the job changes.

## The person may be on the phone
In every mode that fills, fill-only included, assume the person answers through Remote Control and cannot touch Chrome, so never leave a required field for the browser.
- **A complete sheet before any fill.** Pass 1 reads the live form and `J sheet check <key> --live <file>` compares it with the sheet. Read each field's `options` as the real labels of one control, word for word; a field made of several controls (month and year dropdowns, or day, month and year) is one field with `"type":"date"` and `"parts":[{"name","options"}]`. The fill sets each part's control with the value the sheet carries for that part, and the read-back returns each part (`"parts":[{"name","value"}]`). Every required field needs an answer on the sheet (facts, the profile, the records, saved answers, the resume or drafted writing). A required field with none holds the job as `needs_facts` with no fill: ask it in the session first (a plain question for a typed answer, or the form's own options; `J answer add`, then rebuild with `J sheet build <key> --questions <live file>` and check again). In a turn the watcher wakes nobody is asked: the job holds with one notification.
- **Acknowledgments before the fill.** A legal acknowledgment (an arbitration agreement, a privacy notice) is asked with `J ask acknowledge` before the fill, with that job's other questions, and recorded with `J agree <key> <label>`. In the submit modes an attestation is covered by the approval; in the fill-only mode it is asked with the acknowledgments. With the yes the fill ticks both.
- **The read-back reads the form's values** (`read_page` or the form state), never a screenshot alone: `J sheet readback <key> --read <file>`. A field that does not confirm is filled again once; if it still fails the job holds as `fill_problem` and you show the person the field and the value the form has. Never leave a field to be checked later.
- **Optional fields** follow the optional-writing choice; an optional field the profile or the resume answers (a current company, a link) is filled.
- **Finishing from the phone.** The `fill_ready` notice is built by `J ask approval --key <job>`: "Submit it now", "I'll submit in Chrome", "Not this one". "Submit it now" is `J approve <key> --submit-now`; then `J apply next --all` hands the job out again (`fromFilled`) and the browser subagent runs `J may-submit` on the unchanged sheet hash, presses submit, and fills again first and submits in one pass when the tab was closed or the form lost its values. The fill-only mode never reaches a submit without that answer. Just open the pages is unchanged.

## The browser subagent (Agent `model: "haiku"`, one per application)
Put gate 8's rules in this subagent's task: nothing in code sees its clicks. Submit only when `handout.submit` is true and `J may-submit <key> <sheet hash>` answers yes right before the click (it answers no after fill-only, a withdraw, a changed or failing sheet, a read-back that failed, a closed batch, a stop, a job already submitted, an excluded company, or a job that was never handed out; in an unattended batch it also checks the consent and its rules hash, the confirmed dry run, the writing consent, the period limit and the apply mode, and a job gets one pass: then stop and leave the form as it is). A Chrome stop (`chrome_disconnected`, `no_chrome_tools`) closes the batch, attended too.

The fill and the submit run in one browser pass after the yes, never before it (a fill before the yes would mean two fills per job or tabs left open across turns, which a Chrome disconnect drops): it fills from the approved sheet, reads the form back against the sheet, and submits; the approval covers the exact sheet hash, and the read-back against the sheet hash stops any difference before submit. On any system that gives no form fields through an API (Lever, Ashby and every other employer system), a first browser pass at prepare time reads the live form to build the sheet and checks that every sheet field fits it, submitting nothing and closing the tab.

Give it only the handout's URL, the sheet's fields and the resume path. It uses Claude in Chrome tool calls, in its own tab, and:
1. Opens the URL. 2. Reads the form fields. 3. Pass 1: returns the live fields as JSON; the main session runs `J sheet check <key> --live <file>`. A field the sheet lacks, a changed field or a redirect to another host stops the job.
4. Pass 2: fills each field with `form_input` by reference, stages the resume with `J uploads stage <key>` (its reply names the path, in the working folder under the file's own name; if it says nothing was copied, the form is filled without the file and the report says so once), uploads that path with `file_upload`, then `J uploads unstage <key>` on success or failure, reads the values back, at most one screenshot. The main session runs `J sheet readback <key> --read <file>`; a mismatch stops before submit.
5. Submits only when `handout.submit` is true (never in a dry run). Returns a short result: status, filled fields as JSON, problems.

If the subagent has no Chrome tools or fails, hold the job (`J ops` transition `stopped`), report it, and do not retry on another model.

After a submit, `J ops` transition `submitted`; record the URL, filled fields and confirmation text. Say "submitted"; say "confirmed" only when the page or a forwarded email confirms.

## Stops
When a copy or an upload fails (`Cannot upload ...`): `J stop <key> --kind upload_blocked`. It parks only that job, with one report line, and never asks the person anything: the other jobs go on, and in fill-only or open-only the person can attach the file in the form. A bot check or unknown field: `J stop <key> --kind ...`. Chrome gone: `J stop <key> --kind chrome_disconnected` and tell the person to reconnect with `/chrome`. Then `J batch close`. An unattended batch also closes in code when `apply next` finds nothing left to submit, or on a captcha or `chrome_disconnected` stop; a batch with no activity for two hours is closed by the next `J work`.

After the person has dealt with a stop (a captcha solved, a login done), `J unpark <key>` puts the job back in the queue.

## Unattended
New writing needs the writing consent, which is separate from the unattended one. Without it, `apply next` queues the job as `writing_needs_yes` (one notification, no submit): show the draft, and on the person's yes `J writing approve <key>` (add `--edits file.json` for edited text); the fields become earlier answers and the job returns to the queue. Optional writing fields follow the recorded choice: `skip` leaves them empty (`handout.skipFields`), `wait` holds the job. A changed writing rule makes the writing consent stale.

First batch is a dry run (`handout.dryRun`): fill and read back, submit nothing, `J dryrun record <key> '<json>'`. The person reviews, then `J dryrun confirm`. After that, `J apply next --unattended` submits only jobs that pass the rules. After each run give one report.

## Fill-only

For a job with `J fillonly <key> on`, or under a `fill_only` rule (every job, or a named company), `apply next` hands out `submit: false` and `fillOnly: true`. Fill, read back, never submit, leave the tab open, then `J fill done <key>`: one notification, `fill_ready`. The person checks the tab, presses submit and says so; then `J submitted <key>`. No yes is needed for a fill, because nothing is submitted.
